# BIOMOD 2026 Jamboree abstract (draft)

**Team:** Imperial
**Institution:** Imperial College London
**Project title:** FLUX: Tuning a DNA Predator-Prey Oscillator with Molecular Crowding

## Abstract

Every living cell is crowded. Up to a third of its volume is taken up by proteins, nucleic acids and other macromolecules, and this crowding shapes how molecules meet, bind and react. Yet almost every synthetic DNA circuit ever built has been designed and tested in dilute buffer, an environment that exists nowhere in biology. If molecular programs are ever to run inside cells, in artificial cells, or in real-world diagnostic and therapeutic settings, we need to know how they behave when the space around them fills up.

FLUX tackles this question with one of the most striking systems in DNA nanotechnology: the predator-prey oscillator of Fujii and Rondelez, a living-like molecular ecosystem in which three short DNA strands and three enzymes act out the classic dynamics of rabbits and foxes. Prey strands replicate, predator strands grow by consuming them, and an enzyme degrades both, so the two populations rise and fall in sustained waves for hours without any outside input.

We rebuild this ecosystem in a high-throughput plate format and then place it, for the first time to our knowledge, in crowded conditions using two chemically distinct inert polymers. Rather than rewiring the DNA itself, we change only its surroundings, asking whether the environment alone can tune, reshape or even switch off a molecular clock. Alongside the experiments, we have built a mechanistic model of the full reaction network that translates the physics of crowding into testable predictions. Together, FLUX aims to turn crowding from an overlooked nuisance into a design tool for programming molecular behaviour.
